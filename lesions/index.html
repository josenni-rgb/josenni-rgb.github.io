Stage 01

After Your Consult

You have met Dr. Kang about an area in the mouth that needs a closer look. A biopsy or removal lets us study a small sample of tissue so we know exactly how to care for you.

You have met Dr. Kang about an area in the mouth that needs a closer look. A biopsy is a small, planned visit. Your care team will unlock the next step when a date is set.

The path at a glance

  1. A look and a planWe examine the area, talk about whether a small sample or a full removal is recommended, and answer your questions.
  2. Why this visit helpsLooking at tissue under a microscope is how we know what the area is. It is a routine part of careful care.
  3. Procedure dayUsually in the clinic with local anaesthetic. You go home the same day.
  4. A few quiet daysMild soreness is common. Soft food and gentle rinsing are usually enough.
  5. Results by phoneReports typically take 1–3 weeks. We call you to review them and talk about any next step.
  6. We will be in touchWhen the visit is booked, you will get a link for the day of the procedure. Afterward, we call when the report is in.

Waiting can feel long. That wait is normal. You do not need to search the internet for the area we discussed — bring questions to us instead.

Stage 02

Procedure Day

This is usually a short visit in the clinic. You will be numb in the area. Most people go home soon after and continue their day at an easy pace.

  1. Before

    Eat and arrive as usual

    If you are having local anaesthetic only, a normal meal is fine. Fast and arrange a ride only if we have planned sedation — we will have said so clearly.

  2. Visit

    Numb, then a small sample or removal

    We numb the site. A small piece of tissue is taken, or the whole area is removed if that was the plan. Stitches are used when they will help the site rest.

  3. After

    Bite on gauze, then home

    Gentle pressure helps. A little oozing is expected. The sample goes to the laboratory; you do not wait at the clinic for the report.

The report is not ready today. That is expected. We call you when it is, typically in 1–3 weeks.

Stage 03

Recovery

Most people describe this as sore, not severe. Follow the printed aftercare we give you if it differs from this page.

The first days

  • Mild swelling or bruising can appear and then fade.
  • Soft, lukewarm food. Chew away from the site.
  • Steady gauze pressure if you see more than light pink saliva.
  • No straws, smoking, or vaping while the site is tender — often several days.

Keeping it comfortable

  • After 24 hours, gentle salt-water rinses if we asked you to.
  • Brush neighbouring teeth carefully. Skip the site at first.
  • Stitches often dissolve. Do not pull at them.
  • Over-the-counter pain relief as we discussed, following the label.

A small white or yellow patch on the site as it heals can be a normal covering. Call us if the area opens, if bleeding will not slow, or if soreness is growing after the third day.

Stage 04

Results & Follow-up

The laboratory studies the tissue and sends a report to our clinic. We call you to go through it in plain language.

  1. 1–3 weeksTypical wait Some reports come sooner. A few take a little longer if extra stains are needed. That extra time is about being thorough.
  2. We callA conversation, not a portal surprise We review what the report says and whether any follow-up visit is useful.
  3. NextIf anything more is needed We will book it with you. Many people need only the healing they are already doing.
If you have not heard by the end of the third week, please call us at 604-371-0578. Reports can sit in a queue, and we would rather you check in than wait and wonder.

Keep the number we have on file up to date so the call reaches you. If you prefer a specific person with you on the line, tell us when we book the procedure.

Urgent

When to Call Us

Call us, or go to emergency, if any of the following happen. You are not bothering us.

Call us or go to emergency if you have:
  • Heavy bleeding that will not stop with firm gauze pressure for 20 minutes
  • Fever
  • Trouble swallowing or breathing
  • Swelling that gets worse after day 3
  • Numbness of the lip, chin, or tongue that has not resolved as discussed at your visit

Call or text 604-371-0578 any time — during clinic hours (Monday–Friday, 8 AM–4 PM) or after hours (follow the recorded instructions). If you cannot reach us and you are having trouble breathing or bleeding heavily, go to the nearest emergency department.

Help

FAQ

Why is a biopsy recommended?
It lets a laboratory look at a small sample of tissue so we know exactly what the area is and how to care for you. It is a common, careful next step — not a conclusion.
Will the visit hurt?
The site is numbed first. You may feel pressure. Afterward, most people describe mild soreness for a few days, eased with the aftercare we give you.
How long do results take?
Typically 1–3 weeks. We call you to review the report. If you have not heard by the end of the third week, please call us.
Can I go to work the same day?
Many people do, at an easy pace, after a local-anaesthetic visit. If the site is in a spot that makes talking or eating tender, give yourself a quieter afternoon.
Will I have stitches?
Often yes, so the site can rest. They usually dissolve. We will tell you if they need a short return visit.
Who do I call if I am worried?
The clinic at 604-371-0578 — you are welcome to text. After hours, call the same number and follow the recorded instructions. For heavy bleeding, trouble breathing, or trouble swallowing, go to emergency if you cannot reach us.
