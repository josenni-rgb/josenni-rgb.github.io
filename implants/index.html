Stage 01

Consult & CBCT

You have met Dr. Kang. A 3D CBCT scan lets us see bone volume, the sinus, and nearby nerves so we can plan an implant, a graft, or both.

You have met Dr. Kang and had — or will have — a 3D CBCT scan. That scan is how we plan an implant, a graft, or both. Your care team will unlock the next step when it is time.

The path at a glance

  1. Consult and 3D scanWe look at bone, gums, and neighbouring teeth. The CBCT is a focused 3D X-ray of the jaws.
  2. A plan that may include a graftIf bone is thin or a sinus sits low, a graft can create a steadier bed for the implant — sometimes the same day, sometimes as its own step.
  3. Surgery dayThe implant is a small titanium post in the bone. A graft, if you need one, is placed to support that bone.
  4. Quiet months of healingBone typically grows onto the implant over about 3–6 months before the final tooth is made.
  5. Hand-off to your dentistWe do the surgery. Your restoring dentist (or prosthodontist) makes the abutment and crown.
  6. Two teams, one toothOur clinic places the implant and graft. Your dentist restores it when healing is ready.

Why a CBCT scan

A regular X-ray is flat. A CBCT shows height, width, and the space around the site — including the sinus in the upper jaw and the nerve canal in the lower jaw. That is how we choose length, angle, and whether a graft is wise.

Stage 02

Before Surgery

Treat this like any oral-surgery morning. A little setup at home makes the first evening easier.

If you are being sedated

  • Follow the fasting instructions we give you. A common pattern is nothing to eat after midnight, with clear fluids only up to the time we specify.
  • A responsible adult must take you home. You cannot drive after sedation.
  • Take only the medicines we have said you should take.
Local anaesthetic only? A light meal is usually fine, and you may not need a ride. We will tell you which path is yours.

Prescriptions and what to wear

  • Fill prescriptions before the day so they are at home
  • Loose, comfortable clothes; short sleeves if an IV is possible
  • No makeup, jewelry, or contact lenses if you are sedated
  • Health card, photo ID, and your medicine list

At home, set out

  • Ice packs, extra pillows, and soft foods
  • Gauze as we provided
  • If you had a sinus-lift plan: tissues for gentle sniffing — you will not be blowing your nose

Stage 03

Surgery Day

This visit is for the implant, the graft, or both. You typically go home the same day.

  1. Arrive

    Check in

    Confirm your escort if you are sedated. Tell us if your health or medicines have changed.

  2. Visit

    Implant and/or graft

    The gum is numbed. An implant is placed into bone. If a graft is part of the plan, we add material and often a thin membrane to hold it while your own bone grows.

  3. After

    Protect the site

    Bite on gauze as we show you. Do not poke the area with your tongue or a finger. Small granules or a bit of membrane at the edge can appear later — do not pull at them.

  4. Home

    Rest, ice, medicines

    Start ice on the cheek. Take medicines as written. Soft food on the other side of the mouth.

If you had a sinus lift

A sinus lift adds bone under the sinus in the upper back jaw. For the time we specify — often about two weeks, sometimes longer — do not blow your nose. Sneeze with your mouth open. Avoid straws. If you use a CPAP mask, ask us before wearing it over the surgical side.

Particles or membrane: a few grains of graft material on the tongue, or a visible edge of membrane, can happen. Do not pick. Call us if a large piece comes free, if the site opens, or if you are unsure.

Stage 04

Healing & Integration

Bone needs quiet time to grow onto the implant. This stretch is measured in months, not days. It is supposed to feel uneventful.

The first days

  • Swelling often peaks around days 1–3.
  • Ice, head up, soft food, no straws.
  • Keep fingers, toothpicks, and water-flossers away from the site until we say otherwise.
  • If you had a sinus lift: still no nose blowing.

The weeks after

  • Gentle salt-water rinses after 24 hours if we asked you to.
  • Brush neighbouring teeth carefully.
  • A healing cap or stitches may be present. Leave them unless we remove them.
  • Come to the follow-ups we booked — often a short check, then a later visit to test healing.

Typical timing

  1. WeeksSoft-tissue comfort Gums usually feel much better within a couple of weeks.
  2. 3–6 monthsOsseointegration Bone grows onto the implant. Grafts sometimes need the longer end of this range.
  3. ReadyWe let your dentist know When the implant is stable, we hand the next step to your restoring dentist.

A temporary tooth — a flipper, an Essex retainer, or a temporary crown — is arranged with your dentist if you need something in the smile while you wait. Do not chew hard foods on a temporary over a healing implant unless your dentist has said it is designed for that.

Stage 05

Your Restoring Dentist

Our part is the surgery and the heal. The tooth you see and chew with is made by your general dentist or prosthodontist.

  1. We confirm the implant is readyA visit and, when needed, an X-ray. Then we send records to your dentist.
  2. AbutmentA small connector is placed on the implant. This may happen in our clinic or in theirs — we will say which.
  3. Crown, bridge, or dentureYour dentist takes impressions or a scan and makes the final tooth or teeth.
  4. Long-term careClean around the implant as your dentist shows you. Keep both our clinic and your dentist in the loop if something feels different.
Who to call: questions about the crown, bite, or temporary tooth go to your restoring dentist. Questions about the implant, graft, sinus, or surgical site still come to us at [Clinic phone].

Urgent

When to Call Us

Call us, or go to emergency, if any of the following happen. You are not bothering us.

Call us or go to emergency if you have:
  • Heavy bleeding that will not stop with firm gauze pressure for 20 minutes
  • Fever
  • Trouble swallowing or breathing
  • Swelling that gets worse after day 3
  • Numbness of the lip, chin, or tongue that has not resolved as discussed at your visit

Also call us (non-emergency) if graft particles keep coming out in quantity, if a membrane feels loose, or if you cannot keep from blowing your nose after a sinus lift.

Help

FAQ

Why are implants and grafts on the same page?
Recovery is similar: protect the site, keep it clean, and give bone time. A graft may be done with the implant or as its own visit. Your plan will say which.
How long until I have a tooth?
Soft tissue settles in weeks. The implant typically needs about 3–6 months in bone before the final crown. Your dentist makes that tooth after we confirm healing.
What is a sinus lift?
In the upper back jaw, a thin sinus can sit where bone is needed. A sinus lift gently adds room for graft material. Do not blow your nose while it heals, and sneeze with your mouth open.
Something small came out of the site. Is that expected?
A few graft granules, or an edge of membrane, can show. Do not pick. Call us if a large piece comes free or if you are unsure.
Who makes the crown?
Your restoring dentist or prosthodontist. We place the implant and graft; they restore the tooth you chew with.
Who do I call if I am worried?
The clinic at [Clinic phone] during hours. After hours, use [After-hours line]. For heavy bleeding, trouble breathing, or trouble swallowing, go to emergency if you cannot reach us.
