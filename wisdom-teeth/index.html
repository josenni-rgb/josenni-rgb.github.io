What we usually talk about

Wisdom teeth are the last molars at the back of the mouth. They may be partly through the gum, fully under the gum, or pressing on the tooth in front. Imaging helps us see the roots and nearby nerves so the plan is as gentle as it can be.

Stage 02

Before Surgery

A little preparation makes the day smoother. Use this as a checklist the day before and the morning of your visit.

If you are being sedated

  • Follow the fasting instructions we give you. A common pattern is nothing to eat after midnight, with clear fluids only up to the time we specify.
  • Do not drink alcohol the night before.
  • Take only the medicines we have said you should take, with a sip of water if allowed.
  • Arrange a responsible adult to take you home. You cannot drive, bike, or go home alone after sedation.
Local anaesthetic only? You can usually eat a light meal beforehand and may not need a ride. We will tell you which path is yours.

Prescriptions

Fill any prescriptions we give you before surgery day so they are ready at home. Do not start extra medicines or change doses on your own. If a pharmacy has a question, they can call us at 604-371-0578.

What to wear and bring

  • Loose, comfortable clothes with short sleeves if you may have an IV
  • No makeup, jewelry, contact lenses, or nail polish if you are sedated
  • Your health card and a photo ID
  • A list of your medicines and any allergies

At home, set out

  • Ice packs or a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a thin cloth
  • Extra pillows so you can rest with your head up
  • Soft foods: yoghurt, applesauce, lukewarm soup, mashed potato, smoothies without a straw
  • Gauze as we provided, and a phone within reach

Stage 03

Surgery Day

Most wisdom-tooth visits happen here in the clinic. You go home the same day. Arrive a little early so check-in is unhurried.

  1. Arrive

    Check in

    Confirm who is taking you home if you are sedated. Let us know if anything about your health has changed since your consult.

  2. Visit

    The procedure

    You will be numb. If you are sedated, you will feel very relaxed. Time in the chair varies with how many teeth and how they sit — often well under an hour of surgical time, sometimes a little longer.

  3. After

    Bite on gauze

    Steady, gentle pressure helps a clot form. A little oozing on the gauze is expected. We will show you how to change it.

  4. Home

    Go home and rest

    Start ice on the cheeks. Take your medicines as written, before numbness fully fades if we have asked you to. An adult should stay with you if you were sedated.

No straws, no smoking, no vaping, and no spitting for the first week. Those habits can dislodge the clot that protects the site.

Stage 04

Recovery

Healing is gradual. The notes below are a general guide. Follow Dr. Kang’s instructions for you if they differ.

Days 1–3

  • Swelling often grows for the first two or three days. That is expected.
  • Ice the cheeks 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off, while you are awake, for the first day or two.
  • Rest with your head up. No heavy lifting or hard exercise.
  • Soft, cool or lukewarm food. Chew away from the sites. No straws.
  • Light pink saliva is common. Firm pressure with gauze for 20 minutes if you see more blood.

The first week

  • After 24 hours, begin gentle salt-water rinses if we asked you to — do not swish hard.
  • Brush the rest of your teeth carefully. Keep the brush away from the sockets at first.
  • Stitches often dissolve on their own. Do not pull at them.
  • Pain that eases, then sharpens again around days 3–5, can mean the clot was lost. Call us — this is treatable.
  • Bruising of the cheek or neck can appear and then fade.

Weeks 2 and after

  • Most people return to usual meals and work as comfort allows. Bone in the sockets keeps filling in for months.
  • Keep the area clean. Food can collect in the hollows; a gentle rinse after meals helps.
  • Come to any follow-up we booked. If something does not feel right, you do not need to wait for that visit — call us.

2–3

Days off for many desk jobs

Longer if several teeth were removed, if your work is physical, or if you were sedated.

7

Days without straws, smoking, or vaping

These protect the clot. If you use tobacco, this is a good week to pause.

Urgent

When to Call Us

Call us, or go to emergency, if any of the following happen. You are not bothering us.

Call us or go to emergency if you have:
  • Heavy bleeding that will not stop with firm gauze pressure for 20 minutes
  • Fever
  • Trouble swallowing or breathing
  • Swelling that gets worse after day 3
  • Numbness of the lip, chin, or tongue that has not resolved as discussed at your visit

Call or text 604-371-0578 any time — during clinic hours (Monday–Friday, 8 AM–4 PM) or after hours (follow the recorded instructions). If you cannot reach us and you are having trouble breathing or bleeding heavily, go to the nearest emergency department.

Help

FAQ

Do I have to fast?
Only if you are having sedation or a general anaesthetic. Follow the written fasting instructions we give you. If you are having local anaesthetic only, a light meal is usually fine.
Can I drive myself home?
Not if you were sedated. You need a responsible adult to take you home and stay with you. After local anaesthetic only, you can usually drive if you feel well.
When can I eat?
Start with soft, cool foods once the numbness begins to fade. Avoid hot drinks, seeds, nuts, and crunchy chips while the sites are tender. No straws for a week.
What is a dry socket?
If the protective clot is lost, pain can increase a few days after surgery. It is uncomfortable and it is treatable. Call us rather than waiting it out.
When can I go back to work or school?
Many people with desk work take 2–3 days. Allow more time if several teeth were removed or if your job is physical.
Who do I call if I am worried?
The clinic at 604-371-0578 — you are welcome to text. After hours, call the same number and follow the recorded instructions. For heavy bleeding, trouble breathing, or trouble swallowing, go to emergency if you cannot reach us.
